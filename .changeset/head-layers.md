Turn the head by sliding the face under the hair. A head turn (`angleX`, `angleY`) has so far moved the face inside the head on one mesh: the hair outline stays, so the hair on one side is squeezed and on the other stretched, like a lens passing over the picture. Avatars can now carry optional head layers: `face_field` (where a turn slides the picture and how much: flat over the eyes, nose and mouth, nothing at the face contour), `hair_front` (the hair in front of the face as its own layer, above the eyes) and `face_skin` (what lies under that hair). With them the WebGL runtime moves only the face: the hair layer sways and rolls with the head but does not slide, the layer under it shows the forehead, brows and eyes where the face has moved away, and the per-part depth offsets of nose and mouth follow the rig's own `shiftX` / `shiftY`. `tools/build-layers.py --head-plate plate.png --head-footprint mask.png` makes the layers from a clean plate (the same picture without the hair in front of the face, for example from an image model's layer decomposition): the hair is lifted off the plate by difference matting, so the resting avatar is still the source picture, the eye layers are cut from the face without that hair, and `eye0_open_under` / `eye1_open_under` are its open-eye layers. `--head-layers` makes them without a plate, telling hair from skin by colour, for pictures whose hair does not reach the eyes. The tool leaves the layers out, with the reason on stderr, when the picture does not allow them. `base.png` and every layer older runtimes draw are unchanged; avatars without the layers keep their original code path and render exactly as before.
