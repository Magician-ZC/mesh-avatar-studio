Hold parts at the pointer, and pull a cheek past the face's contour. `createMeshAvatar` gains `grab(target, x, y)`, `moveGrab(handle, dx, dy, depth?)`, `releaseGrab(handle, vx?, vy?)`, `canGrab(target)` and the boolean `cheekPull` (WebGL runtime only). A grab returns a positive handle, or null when the avatar cannot hold the target; the part follows the pointer's offset from the press and keeps its shape while the pointer rests, and a release hands it back to its own dynamics with a bounded velocity. Targets: a hair `strand` (held at its node nearest the press, within the strand's reach), a garment's hem (`cloth`, leaning towards the pointer within its own hem travel, never lifted), a `limb` chain (swing and bend within its limits), a hanging tassel (`accessory`) and a `cheek` (`side: 'left' | 'right'`, image sides). `tools/build-layers.py --cheek-pull` traces each side's visible outer face contour below the eyes and writes `cheek_left` / `cheek_right` (the cheek's skin from the contour inwards, with its drawn line) and `cheeks` in `layers.json` (the contour, how far into the face a pull reaches at each point, short of the eyes, nose, mouth and chin, and the largest pull). The runtime draws a cheek layer only while that cheek is pulled, over the base and under the eyes, the drawn mouths and the hair in front of the face, and moves its contour only outwards in an arc, so the old contour and the hair beside the face stay covered; a pull inwards or along the contour moves the skin inside it, and depth swells and lights the skin around the press. With head layers the contour's own drawn line moves from `hair_front` to `face_skin` (the same picture at rest), so it goes with the cheek while hair in front stays; without them, hair over a cheek shortens its travel or leaves that side out. Blinks, gaze, the mouth and head turns work while a cheek is pulled. Avatars without the new layers, and older runtimes, render exactly as before.
