Move the iris as a sprite over an eye white of its own. The eye ball layer holds the eye white and the iris in one picture, and a gaze has so far warped that picture inside the opening. On an eye whose iris is as tall as the opening (most semi-realistic eyes) the warp shears the iris, and near the lids and corners it reads beyond the cut-out, so the filled base shows through. A rig eye can now carry an optional `iris` ellipse (the iris outline, also where the lids cover it). For such an eye `tools/build-layers.py` also cuts `eye{i}_white` (the eye ball with the iris painted out from the white beside it, shaded near the lids like that white, carried on beyond the opening) and `eye{i}_iris` (the iris with the white around it up to plain eye white, continued under the lids from its own rings), and the WebGL runtime draws the eye from the two: the white stays, the iris moves by the gaze as it is, clipped by the lids. With `--open-eyes` the open-eye layer of such an eye has its hole exactly where the eye layers draw, so no strip of the iris stays in it; along the lids, where the picture blends the iris with the lid or lash over it, the blend is taken apart: the sprite holds the iris, the open-eye layer the lid's share. At rest the layers over each other are the picture, at any display size (the runtime draws the eye layers a few pixels under the open-eye layer and leaves the line layers out while it is fully shown, so its filtered edge shows nothing but the eye). The tool leaves the pair out, with the reason on stderr, when the picture does not allow it. `eye{i}_ball` is still written and required; eyes without the two layers, and older runtimes, render exactly as before.
